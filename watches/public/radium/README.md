# The Radium Girls' pictures

Each picture keeps its own licence (`README.md` at the repo root, "License"), and
its metadata is removed. The Questions slide in `slides.md` credits them.

- `painters.jpg`: a dial painter at a British clock factory, painting the dial of
  an Ingersoll Crown alarm clock, by Leslie Cardew for the _Daily Herald_,
  January 14, 1932. The Science Museum Group holds the Daily Herald Archive:
  [its record of the print](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co8801010/daily-herald-photograph-clock-making)
  (1983-5236/47273), "Hand painting luminous paint on dials". Its licence is not
  open: the Science Museum Group credits it "© Mirrorpix/SMG Images". So it does
  not meet the root `README.md`'s rule; the talk's author chose to use it
  (`tasks/2026-11-15-dial-painter-photo/PLAN.md`, "The rights").
  The file is from the scan of negative A4765 that the Science & Society Picture
  Library made in 2003, 3508 × 2662 px, at
  [the image host of a History.com article](https://res.cloudinary.com/aenetworks/image/upload/v1/102730389_r1mtgc).
  It is a crop of the slide's box, 2526 × 2000 px at 235, 300, without the
  plate's border, the negative's number and the broken emulsion at the bottom.
  Then it is grey, scaled to 1080 px tall (JPEG quality 85).
  `tasks/2026-11-15-dial-painter-photo/evidence/crop.sh` makes it. Not an
  oversight: the Science Museum Group's own scan (6468 × 5214 px) is of the
  newspaper's print, which was retouched in 1932. The two women behind her and
  the window are painted out, and an editor's marks cross her hair. The Commons
  copy, `Las chicas del radio pintando.jpg`, is a smaller crop (1152 × 625 px) of
  the negative's scan.
- `fryer.jpg`: [Grace Fryer](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Grace_Fryer.jpg),
  unknown photographer, 1925, at the size of its source. Public domain.
