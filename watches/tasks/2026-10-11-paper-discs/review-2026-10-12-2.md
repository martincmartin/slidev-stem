## What is good

### One rendering path preserves the established line rule

The face pass writes a holdout ID. The line pass obtains that holdout’s depth from its plane.

Ordinary faces keep their established depth tolerance. This preserves the behavior of curved walls, overlays, and decals.

The shader’s separate holdout and ordinary-face tests are necessary. Combining them would change the measured visual contract.

The watch has eight holdouts. The renderer supports 32 and checks that limit before it creates context resources.

At 1960 × 1104, the R8 target holds approximately 2.06 MiB of data. The previous RG32F target held approximately 16.51 MiB. This reduces that target’s storage by 87.5%.

These are calculated texture-data sizes, not measured GPU allocations.

### Resource and scene ownership remain clear

Holdout IDs and plane corners belong to renderer-side data. They do not change the scene’s fixed meshes.

The renderer recomputes planes for each picture. Thus, a group can move while its holdout geometry remains fixed.

The holdout texture follows the existing allocation, resize, cleanup, and context-restoration paths. The renderer detaches sampled attachments before it draws lines.

`checkHoldout()` rejects dynamic geometry. This makes cached plane corners safe.

### Geometry and choreography remain separate

A paper disc belongs to its wheel’s piece. The normal piece logic controls its visibility and fades.

The disc uses the stationary watch group because clipping can make it noncircular. This preserves the oversized wheel’s relationship to the case and crown.

Holdouts retain the paper colour during dimming and flashes. They do not enlarge fitted piece extents.

The implementation preserves pinion contact across the small stack clearance. The tests check these heights directly.

No slide choreography, Vue component, or still-state implementation changed in this diff.

### The test helper has a justified responsibility

`AtRest` supplies world-space geometry to both geometry guards. `Outline` supplies the surface intersection calculation.

The helper’s bounding-box and inner-circle shortcuts avoid unnecessary clipping. Its new tests cover height interpolation, crossing faces, outside faces, and boundary contact.

Keeping this helper separate from production generation is appropriate. It does not put test state on scene objects.

## Findings and local improvements

### 1. P3 — Complete the outline’s claimed input check

**Locations:** `watch/at-rest.ts:74`, `watch/at-rest.ts:93`, and `watch/at-rest.test.ts:51`.

The constructor checks whether its averaged centre lies inside every edge half-plane. This does not establish that the polygon is convex.

I tested this concave outline:

```text
(0, 0), (10, 0), (10, 10), (6, 8), (0, 10)
```

The constructor accepted it. `heightsOver()` then returned `null` for this face inside the polygon:

```text
(7, 8, 0), (8, 8, 0), (8, 9, 1)
```

The clipper intersects the edge half-planes. For a concave polygon, that intersection does not represent the polygon.

The test named “not convex, or too thin” supplies duplicate points. It checks a degenerate outline, not this concavity case.

Current generated discs are convex. This finding does not show a failure in their geometry. It concerns a reusable helper that claims to reject invalid input.

**Alternatives, ranked:**

1. **Validate the convex input explicitly.** Check finite points, nonzero edges, and the required convex winding. Add the demonstrated concavity case.
2. **Narrow the helper’s contract.** Make convexity a stated caller precondition and remove the unsupported rejection claim.
3. **Support concave polygons.** Reject this option for the present task. It adds geometry machinery that neither caller needs.

The first option best matches the project’s input-contract rules. It changes one constructor and its existing test file.

A direct all-vertices-against-all-edges check needs about 16,384 comparisons for a 128-point outline. It runs once per outline. I did not measure its runtime.

### 2. Reuse `transformPoint()` in `AtRest.points()`

**Locations:** `watch/at-rest.ts:35` and `webgl/math.ts:26`.

`AtRest.points()` repeats the existing affine point transform. Its local `row()` function computes the same three matrix rows as `transformPoint()`.

I compared both calculations across all 276,831 triangle vertices in the current watch’s 91 meshes. Every coordinate was exactly equal.

**Recommended change:** Import `transformPoint()` and call it for each vertex.

This affects one method in one file. It replaces the seven-line local calculation with the existing helper. It adds no abstraction or dependency.

**Alternatives:**

- Keep the duplicate calculation: lowest immediate cost, but two implementations remain.
- Add a general matrix helper: unnecessary; the required helper already exists.

The existing tests and the same exact-coordinate comparison provide appropriate preservation checks.

Do not apply this substitution blindly to `windowPoint()`. That function also handles homogeneous division and conversion to window coordinates.

### 3. Make the new geometry names describe their objects and values

**Locations:** `webgl/renderer.ts:1409`, `watch/generate.ts:909`, and `watch/generate.ts:2638`.

Three small naming changes would reduce the work needed to understand the assembly:

- In `holdoutPlanes()`, rename `a` and `b` to depth-slope names. The project rules require descriptive local names.
- In `PaperDisc`, rename `wheel` to `coveringGroup`. It holds a `Group`, while nearby `Round.wheel` holds a wheel object.
- Rename the temporary `under` association to `wheelMesh` or another explicit ownership name. It identifies which piece must receive the disc.

The plane rename affects one function and its coefficient references.

The `PaperDisc` rename affects one private interface, three construction sites, and three member reads. The ownership rename affects three statements in generation.

These changes preserve the arithmetic, mesh selection, and insertion order.

**Alternatives, ranked:**

1. Rename these local fields and values.
2. Keep the names and add more comments.
3. Introduce wrapper objects for groups, wheels, or ownership.

Prefer renaming. The required relationships already exist; wrappers would add structure without removing uncertainty.

## What to leave alone

### Keep the separate polygon clippers

`generate.ts` clips 2D polygons to build meshes. `at-rest.ts` clips 3D triangles to measure heights, with an inward margin.

They use related algorithms, but their contracts differ. Sharing them would need interpolation and boundary-policy parameters.

For two small callers, that generalized interface would make the calculation harder to follow. Keep both implementations and their purpose-specific tests.

### Keep the holdout helpers beside the renderer

`planeCorners()`, `windowPoint()`, and `holdoutPlanes()` serve one renderer feature.

Their current separation follows the calculation: select stable corners, project them, then solve the plane. A new plane service or shader framework would add unnecessary interfaces.

Keep the formula and attachment sequence intact.

### Keep generation’s mesh-to-piece assembly

The current assembly connects discs, meshes, pieces, and extents in one place.

Do not replace it with another ownership registry or callback system. The existing object relationships and tests already establish the required behavior.

### Keep the single R8 path

The previous extension branch is gone. Do not reintroduce alternatives for machines or scenes unless measurements establish a need.

Allocating the R8 target only for scenes with holdouts would add framebuffer and shader branches. This talk’s only scene has holdouts.

### Do not split files merely because they are long

The current renderer has 1,522 lines; generation has 3,226. The diff adds a net 140 and 88 lines respectively.

Those counts help describe scope. They do not establish a useful module boundary.

The existing sections keep the renderer’s state management and the watch’s assembly visible. I found no evidence that a broad split would make this change easier to maintain.
