## The sixth message

> The Pre-1850 Cost Bottleneck for Precision: By the 1830s and 1840s, the
> detached-lever escapement and jeweling allowed pocket watches to keep time
> within seconds per day, but European cottage artisans still hand-filed and
> custom-fitted every gear train and escapement (Landes, 1979). Because the
> European putting-out system had hit the technical ceiling of hand-fitted
> tolerances, a truly accurate lever watch remained an expensive bespoke
> instrument costing $40 to $150 or more, while cheap imported watches
> remained unreliable and costly to repair (Carosso, 1949; Kelly & Ó Gráda,
> 2016; Stephens, 1989).
>
> That's the source for Waltham creating accurate watches that are
> affordable, everyday instruments for railroad workers, soldiers, and the
> general public. General public/middle class couldn't afford accurate
> watches before 1850, they weren't everyday items for them.
